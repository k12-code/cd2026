01   Executive Summary
Key Insights
Complaints - a reliable churn signal

Users who complain churn at 14.6% (dx=7) vs 7.9% for non-complainers — consistently ~1.9× higher across all three months. At D45, the retention gap between complainers and non-complainers is 10–16 percentage points across mid-tenure cohorts. This is not a coincidence; it is a behavioural pattern that repeats across every age segment above 30 days.

The intervention window is 91–180 days — not new users

The D45 retention gap peaks at 91–120 days (14.9pp) and stays high through 151–180 days (14.0pp). These users have enough tenure to be worth retaining, but have not yet reached the loyalty threshold where complaints stop mattering. New users (0–30 days) churn heavily regardless of whether they complain — complaint-driven intervention has limited impact there.

02   Definitions
Metrics used in the report
Total Users
Total Deliveries from Core Metrics. One delivery = one active user day. Used as the denominator for complaint rate.
Complaining Users
Distinct users who raised at least one complaint on a given day, from the Retention Matrix. Different from complaint ticket count — one user can raise multiple tickets.
Churn (dx=7)
A user is churned if they do not place an order within 7 days of their complaint date. This is the active churn window used throughout.
Retention (D14/D21/D33/D45)
Share of users from a daily cohort still active at day 14, 21, 33, or 45. Reported as daily average rate across all 90 days (Dec–Feb).
Refund Rate
Refunded complaints ÷ Total complaints. Measures the share of complaints that resulted in a refund.
Reopen Rate
Re-opened complaints ÷ Total complaints. A complaint that is closed and then re-raised by the user within the period.

Hypothesis validated — with one important exception

The 6.7pp churn delta is consistent across Dec, Jan, and Feb, ruling out seasonality or volume effects. Complaints are a reliable predictor of short-window churn.

Exception — 0–30 day users: In the newest user cohort, non-complainers actually churn more (30.2%) than complainers (27.2%). This is because very new users who complain have demonstrated enough engagement to notice a problem and raise it — the larger silent majority churn passively before reaching that point. Complaint-driven intervention is therefore of limited incremental value for sub-30-day users; lifecycle onboarding is the primary lever there.

Corroborated by Play Store sentiment — 2,656 reviews, Dec 2025 – Apr 2026

The same users the churn numbers are counting are leaving a public record. In 1,287 one-star Play Store reviews, the language is explicit about intent to leave — these are not passive churners.

TAT improved but reopen rate is not falling fast enough

Average TAT improved from 11.5h (Dec) to 10.5h (Feb). The overall reopen rate has been stable at 8.0–8.4% across all three months. TAT and reopen rate are moving independently — faster response is not automatically reducing the share of complaints being re-raised. Speed of first response alone is insufficient to reduce churn.

TAT improving — reopen rate flat
Dec 2025 → Feb 2026. TAT: 11.5h → 10.3h → 10.5h (↓9%). Reopen rate: 8.4% → 7.9% → 8.0% — flat despite TAT improvement.
Resolution speed and public rating — a second data source

Across 2,656 Play Store reviews (Dec 2025 – Apr 2026), resolution time follows a clear pattern by star rating. Every 4-star and 5-star review had its ticket resolved within 24 hours. 17–22% of 1–2 star reviews had resolutions taking over 48 hours. This is an independent dataset — no refund or churn data involved — and it points to the same conclusion: resolution speed has a floor effect, but the ceiling is set by resolution quality, not speed.

07   Recommendations
Action items
Grounded in the data above — each item tied to a specific finding
1
Build a complaint-triggered intervention for 91–180 day users
The D45 retention gap peaks at 91–120 days (14.9pp) and remains high through 151–180 days (14.0pp). Users in this tenure band who complain retain at only 36–46% at D45, vs 51–60% for non-complainers in the same band. These are users with enough history to be worth retaining and enough loyalty gap to be recoverable. A structured outreach — proactive call, compensation offer, or account review — triggered within 24–48h of a complaint from this cohort is the single highest-leverage retention action available.
Retention · CRM trigger
2
Audit and restructure B&C complaint resolution process
B&C has the fastest TAT profile of all categories (92.1% of complaints responded to within 24h) — so the problem is not response speed. The 9.1% reopen rate is the signal: nearly 1 in 11 B&C complaints is closed and re-raised by the user. A review of what the team is closing these complaints as, and whether that outcome matches what users expected, is needed before any further TAT or volume work will have churn impact.

"After activating auto-pay, they started recharging on their own will. Customer care replied after 5 days that I had changed the settings — while I have all the proofs." · "MRP on the product is ₹101 and I was charged ₹110. Closing my complaint without any resolution is not the right way." Source: Play Store 1-star reviews, Billing & Collection category · 34 reviews, avg rating 1.2
Urgent · B&C ops
3
Decouple TAT improvement from resolution quality tracking
TAT improved from 11.5h to 10.5h Dec→Feb. The reopen rate held flat at 8.0–8.4% across all three months. TAT is being measured and improved; resolution quality needs its own tracking. Introduce a resolution quality metric — e.g. complaint closed without reopen within 14 days — tracked monthly alongside TAT, so future TAT investment can be validated against actual churn impact.
Measurement · Ops
4
Deprioritise complaint-driven intervention for sub-30-day users
0–30 day non-complainers churn at 30.2% — higher than 0–30 day complainers at 27.2%. Churn in this cohort is driven by lifecycle factors (first-order experience, product fit, habit formation) rather than complaint resolution. Complaint-specific interventions have near-zero marginal impact here and should not be prioritised over broader onboarding and early engagement programmes for new users.
