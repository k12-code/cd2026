01 · Category Retention Analysis & Insights
So whatMP and Snacks are the highest-signal retention categories in the portfolio — but combined, they reach only 6.1% of week-1 users. The constraint is exposure, not loyalty.
Problem
A consistent framework was required to track Category-level retention — a standardised way to compare buyer vs non-buyer behaviour across categories and understand which signals were reliable vs noise.
Approach
Built a cohort analysis framework: 154 daily cohorts across 7 categories (Milk, MP, CCW, FnV, Ghee, Snacks, Staples), D0–D28 retention windows, buyer vs non-buyer delta as the primary signal metric. 7-day and 28-day moving averages to smooth daily noise.

02 · D7 Inflection Analysis — Why Signals Moved
So whatMost retention swings trace back to a specific pricing or assortment decision — Milk price hikes, a Ghee SKU discontinuation, CCW price cuts. Snacks is the one exception: its swing is structural, not engineered.
Problem
The category retention dashboard showed when delta spikes and troughs occurred — but not why. SKU-level attribution was needed to know whether to replicate or avoid the conditions that caused each inflection.
Approach
Pre/post analysis for inflection points across all 7 categories. For each event: identified the date, the magnitude of the delta shift, and traced it to a specific pricing change, SKU discontinuation, assortment event, or seasonal factor using order-level data.

03 · Complaints & Churn Impact Analysis
So whatComplaining behaves as a churn signal, strongest at 61–180 days — not a resolution-speed problem.
Problem
To check whether complaint behaviour signals elevated churn risk — and where in the user lifecycle that signal is strongest. Read as a signal, not a cause: complaining likely reflects pre-existing dissatisfaction rather than driving churn by itself.
Approach
Built using Core Metrics, TAT bucket data, Retention Matrix CSVs, in-app post-delivery ratings (70,612), and Play Store reviews (2,656). Analysed D45 retention gap across 14 age buckets for complainers vs non-complainers.

04 · To increase MP adoption — Interstitial Design & A/B Test
InsightThe combined interstitial test arm changed three things at once, so the lift can't be cleanly attributed to the interstitial alone — but two of four signals (Listicle, B&B CTR) hold up under real significance testing. Details provided over the next few slides.
Problem
MP has the most stable retention signal in the portfolio but only reaches 5.3% of week-1 users. Effective Early journey comms were required to convert MP non-buyers.
Hypothesis
Leading with reason-to-believe (RTB) before the offer would attract committed buyers rather than discount-hunters, improving conversion quality even if broad CTR appears lower.
Experiment
RTB-first interstitial variants were designed and deployed via MoEngage (Android/iOS × Telugu/non-Telugu). Test: digits 0,1 · Control: digits 2,3. Primary metric: MP DCC per digit.
Sources Interstitial variants FO Comms sheet MP Journey Summary MP SKU Summary Testing video
04 · MP Interstitial — What Changed In Test
What changed in test
Test digits received the new MP "Most Loved" interstitial — RTB-first with product grid (Taaza Paneer, Ghar Jaisa Dahi, Idli Batter, Breads, Eggs). Control saw the existing B&B interstitial. But test digits also got a shortened 4+4 banner, a nested listicle layout, and a different trigger/frequency (after 1 delivery at 10x, vs control's after-3-deliveries at 6x) — all at the same time. So the overall MP lift can't be cleanly attributed to the interstitial alone; each piece is broken out separately over the next two slides.

Why DCC matters more than CTR%
Primary metric: MP delivery count (DCC) per digit — more MP buyers is what ultimately moves retention, not the per-impression click rate. CTR% alone can mislead: a campaign shown to a broader audience will naturally have lower per-impression rate. Absolute buyer count > conversion rate. This framing was key to correctly reading the combined interstitial signal.

04 · MP Interstitial — Note & Open Items
What's showing negative
Combined interstitial — significantly worse on both metrics vs control: −1.80pp conversion (p<0.0001) and −1.70pp CTR (p<0.0001). Test arm is also ~2.6× the population size of control here, so this isn't a small-sample fluke.
What the data actually shows
Higher-intent control users are converting better on Combined — consistent with what we saw earlier (control skews toward users already 3 deliveries in, vs test's 1-delivery trigger).
Post-launch trend (Telugu + non-Telugu combined, since Jun 5): conversion has moved from 12.5% (Jun 3) down to 5.8% (Jun 14), CTR from 23.0% to 14.5%. This is a single rolled-out arm — the decline looks like normal novelty decay after launch, but it's early (9 days) and worth tracking before treating either end of that range as the stable number.
SKU signals: Taaza Paneer 180g (+47.1%) and Ghar Jaisa Dahi 400g (+14.5%) winning with lower discount than decliners. Quality and trust signal stronger than price for MP — consistent with RTB-first design intent.

05 · Prototyping early activation — App Wireframe Redesign
Problem
The current app surfaces Milk prominently but can further use the week-1 flow to introduce MP or Snacks — the two highest-signal retention categories that reach only 6.1% of new users combined.
Hypothesis
Redesigning the screens to anchor on Milk, cross-sell MP, and introduce Snacks as a discovery trigger — with specific offer constraints (4+4 Milk, ₹0 FnV) — would increase week-1 category breadth without disrupting the Milk-first experience.
What was built
Screen 1 (New user): Milk 4+4 offer hero → MP cross-sell → Snacks discovery strip → FnV freebie.
Screen 2 (Home): Milk subscription banner → MP product grid → Snacks discovery.
Screen 3 (Snacks): No palm oil / high protein RTB → buy-more-save-more → cross-sell nudge.
Sources Wireframes
05 · Wireframe Redesign — Rationale & Next Step
Design rationale
Each screen decision is grounded in the retention data — Milk as the funnel anchor, MP as the highest-stability cross-sell, Snacks as the highest-signal but lowest-reach category that needs a discovery moment in the early journey.
What was kept intact
  • 4+4 Milk offer structure (pay 4 days, get 4 free) — not changed
  • ₹0 FnV trial offer preserved as a discovery bait
  • Milk subscription as the primary conversion goal
  • MP positioned as nutritional cross-sell, not a competing anchor
Next step to validate
The wireframes are a prototype. Need to analyse: does the MP cross-sell on Screen 1 complement Milk conversion? Does Snacks discovery in early journey move the 0.83% buyer share needle? These need to be answered through an A/B test of the onboarding flow.

What this points to
01
Map the full MP communication journey
From first-order trigger to 180-day retention — every touchpoint where the right message at the right moment could move the needle. The interstitial is one moment in that journey. The 61–180 day window is another. These need to be designed as a sequence, rather than individual experiments.
02
Test Snacks reach as a retention lever
Snacks has the highest D28 delta in the portfolio at 11.9pp — but 0.83% D7 buyer share. The wireframes push Snacks into early journey activation. The question is whether discovery, pricing, or a specific SKU is the unlock. This is the highest-leverage untested intervention in the data.
