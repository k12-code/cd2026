01   Executive Summary
Key Insights
Complaints - as a reliable churn signal

Users who complain churn at 14.6% (dx=7) vs 7.9% for non-complainers — ~1.9× higher, consistent across all three months. At D45, the retention gap between complainers and non-complainers is 10–16 percentage points across mid-tenure cohorts. This pattern holds across every age segment above 30 days.

The intervention window is 91–180 days — not new users

The D45 retention gap peaks at 91–120 days (14.9pp) and stays high through 151–180 days (14.0pp). In-app ratings confirm the same pattern — 91–120 day users give an average rating of 2.48 vs 3.09 for 0–30 day users, with 55% giving 1 star. New users (0–30 days) churn heavily regardless of complaints; early-lifecycle factors dominate there.

Resolution quality is not tracking TAT improvement

TAT improved from 11.5h to 10.5h Dec→Feb. The overall reopen rate held flat at 8.0–8.4% across all three months. Faster response is not reducing the share of complaints being re-raised. B&C specifically has the fastest TAT profile (92.1% responded within 24h) yet a 9.1% reopen rate — the problem is resolution outcome, not speed.

02   Definitions
Metrics used in this report
Total Users
Total Deliveries from Core Metrics. One delivery = one active user day. Denominator for complaint rate.
Complaining Users
Distinct users who raised at least one complaint on a given day (Retention Matrix). One user can raise multiple tickets.
Churn (dx=7)
User did not place an order within 7 days of their complaint date.
Retention D14/D21/D33/D45
Share of a daily cohort still active at day 14, 21, 33, or 45. Daily average rate across all 90 days.
Refund Rate
Refunded complaints ÷ Total complaints. Share of complaints that resulted in a refund.
Reopen Rate
Re-opened complaints ÷ Total complaints. Complaints closed and then re-raised by the user.

04   Causality Test
Do complaints drive churn?
Complainers vs non-complainers — same period, same base
Out of every 100 users — who churns?
Churn = dx=7. Full period average. Complainers churn at 1.9× the rate of non-complainers.
Hypothesis validated — with one important exception

The 6.7pp churn delta is consistent across Dec, Jan, and Feb, ruling out seasonality or volume effects. Complaints are a reliable predictor of short-window churn.

Exception — 0–30 day users: Non-complainers churn more (30.2%) than complainers (27.2%) in this cohort. Very new users who complain have demonstrated enough engagement to notice a problem — the larger silent majority churn passively. Complaint-driven intervention has limited incremental value for sub-30-day users.

Ratings and churn align at the same age zone

The 91–120 day bucket has the worst mid-tenure avg rating (2.48), the highest 1★ share among mid-tenure users (55%), and the widest D45 retention gap (14.9pp). The 120+ bucket has the lowest avg rating overall (2.19) and 62.5% 1★ — long-tenure users who are still complaining are the most dissatisfied cohort in both datasets. Feb was the worst month across all buckets — avg rating fell from 3.09 (Dec, 0–30 days) to 2.48 (91–120 days) and 2.19 (120+).

08   Recommendations
Action items
Each tied to a specific finding in the data above
1
Build a complaint-triggered intervention for 91–180 day users
The D45 retention gap peaks at 91–120 days (14.9pp) and stays high through 151–180 days (14.0pp). In-app ratings confirm the same zone — 91–120 day users avg 2.48 rating with 55% giving 1 star. These users have enough tenure to be worth retaining and enough loyalty gap to be recoverable. A structured outreach — proactive call, compensation offer, or account review — triggered within 24–48h of a complaint from this cohort is the highest-leverage retention action available.
Retention · CRM trigger
2
Audit B&C complaint resolution process
B&C has the fastest TAT profile (92.1% responded within 24h) — response speed is not the problem. The 9.1% reopen rate is the signal: nearly 1 in 11 B&C complaints is closed and re-raised. A review of what the team is closing these complaints as, and whether that matches what users expected, is needed before any further TAT or volume work will have churn impact. "After activating auto-pay, they started recharging on their own will. Customer care replied after 5 days that I had changed the settings — while I have all the proofs." · "MRP on the product is ₹101 and I was charged ₹110. Closing my complaint without any resolution is not the right way." Source: Play Store 1★ reviews, Billing & Collection · 34 reviews, avg rating 1.2
Urgent · B&C ops
3
Decouple TAT tracking from resolution quality tracking
TAT improved from 11.5h to 10.5h Dec→Feb. The reopen rate held flat at 8.0–8.4%. TAT is being measured and improved; resolution quality needs its own metric. Introduce a resolution quality metric — e.g. complaint closed without reopen within 14 days — tracked monthly alongside TAT, so future TAT investment can be validated against actual churn impact.
Measurement · Ops
4
Deprioritise complaint-driven intervention for sub-30-day users
0–30 day non-complainers churn at 30.2% — higher than 0–30 day complainers at 27.2%. Churn in this cohort is driven by lifecycle factors, not complaints. In-app ratings for 0–30 day users are the highest (avg 3.09) — the product experience is not the issue here either. Complaint-specific interventions have near-zero marginal impact; early onboarding and habit-formation programmes are the right lever.
