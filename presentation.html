01 · Category Retention Analysis & Insights
Problem
A consistent framework was required to track Category-level retention — a standardised way to compare buyer vs non-buyer behaviour across categories and understand which signals were reliable vs noise.
Approach

02 · D7 Inflection Analysis — Why Signals Moved
Problem
The category retention dashboard showed when delta spikes and troughs occurred — but not why. SKU-level attribution was needed to know whether to replicate or avoid the conditions that caused each inflection.
Approach
Pre/post analysis for inflection points across all 7 categories. For each event: identified the date, the magnitude of the delta shift, and traced it to a specific pricing change, SKU discontinuation, assortment event, or seasonal factor using order-level data.
Sep 1 · Delta peak +10.3pp
Simultaneous SP increases on Buffalo 450ml and Cow 450ml filtered out low-intent buyers. Pattern repeated throughout — delta strengthens after price hikes, weakens after discount waves.
D7 delta is partly a pricing selection proxy, not pure retention lift
Milk loyalty is real. But buyer share dropped 18.4% → 15.9% — fewer new users buying Milk in week 1. Acquisition problem, not retention.

04 · To increase MP adoption — Interstitial Design & A/B Test
Problem
MP has the most stable retention signal in the portfolio but only reaches 5.3% of week-1 users. Effective Early journey comms were required to convert MP non-buyers.
Hypothesis
Leading with reason-to-believe (RTB) before the offer would attract committed buyers rather than discount-hunters, improving conversion quality even if broad CTR appears lower.
Experiment
RTB-first interstitial variants were designed and deployed via MoEngage (Android/iOS × Telugu/non-Telugu). Test: digits 0,1 · Control: digits 2,3. Primary metric: MP DCC per digit.
Early Results
CTR: 21% non-Telugu, 18% Telugu. Test DCC 176 vs ctrl 159 (+17). Lift is subscription-driven — one-time flat. Taaza Paneer +25.6%, Ghar Jaisa Dahi +10.9%.
What changed in test
Test digits received the new MP "Most Loved" interstitial — RTB-first with product grid (Taaza Paneer, Ghar Jaisa Dahi, Idli Batter, Breads, Eggs). Control saw existing B&B interstitial. The 4+4 banner height was also shortened in test — making MP listicles visible in the first viewport. That change is about Milk, not MP, so any MP delta from it is incremental visibility gain.

05 · Prototyping early activation — App Wireframe Redesign
Problem
The current app surfaces Milk prominently but can further use the week-1 flow to introduce MP or Snacks — the two highest-signal retention categories that reach only 6.1% of new users combined.
Hypothesis
Redesigning the screens to anchor on Milk, cross-sell MP, and introduce Snacks as a discovery trigger — with specific offer constraints (4+4 Milk, ₹0 FnV) — would increase week-1 category breadth without disrupting the Milk-first experience.
What was built
Screen 1 (New user): Milk 4+4 offer hero → MP cross-sell → Snacks discovery strip → FnV freebie.
Screen 2 (Home): Milk subscription banner → MP product grid → Snacks discovery.
Screen 3 (Snacks): No palm oil / high protein RTB → buy-more-save-more → cross-sell nudge.
Design rationale
Each screen decision is grounded in the retention data — Milk as the funnel anchor, MP as the highest-stability cross-sell, Snacks as the highest-signal but lowest-reach category that needs a discovery moment in week 1.
What was kept intact
  • 4+4 Milk offer structure (pay 4 days, get 4 free) — not changed
  • ₹0 FnV trial offer preserved as a discovery bait
  • Milk subscription as the primary conversion goal
  • MP positioned as nutritional cross-sell, not a competing anchor
Next step to validate
The wireframes are a prototype — not tested with users yet. The key questions the design raises: does the MP cross-sell on Screen 1 complement Milk conversion? Does Snacks discovery in week 1 move the 0.83% buyer share needle? These need to be answered through an A/B test of the onboarding flow.
What this points to
01
Map the full MP communication journey
From first-order trigger to 180-day retention — every touchpoint where the right message at the right moment could move the needle. The interstitial is one moment in that journey. The 91–180 day window is another. These need to be designed as a sequence, not individual experiments.
02
Test Snacks reach as a retention lever
Snacks has the highest D28 delta in the portfolio at 11.9pp — but 0.83% D7 buyer share. The wireframes push Snacks into week-1 activation. The question is whether discovery, pricing, or a specific SKU is the unlock. This is the highest-leverage untested intervention in the data.
03
Understand why Milk buyer share is narrowing
D7 buyer share fell from 18.4% to 15.9% while buyer retention held flat. The data shows the what, not the why. Is it a discovery problem, pricing, or cohort composition shift? The answer determines whether the fix is a product change, a comms change, or an acquisition strategy change.
